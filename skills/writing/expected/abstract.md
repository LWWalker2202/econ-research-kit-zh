# Abstract

Whether high-speed rail reshapes the skill composition of local labour demand is the question this paper asks. The identification exploits staggered station openings across Chinese prefectures within a difference-in-differences design with prefecture and year fixed effects. Establishment-level records cover five years before and after each opening. The estimate implies that an opening raises the share of workers with tertiary education by about [待核实] percentage points within five years. The effect appears in tradable services and in establishments that recruit outside the local market, which points to migration rather than local training as the main channel. The paper also shows that the estimate is unchanged when openings that coincided with a national pilot are dropped. These results connect transport infrastructure to the allocation of skill across places.

**Keywords:** high-speed rail; skill composition; difference-in-differences; labour demand
