# Response to the editor and the referees

We thank the editor and the two referees for a careful reading of the paper. The revision adds industry-clustered standard errors, an event-study figure on the full sample window, a discussion of how migration is measured, and a new appendix table on the sensitivity of the preferred specification. The main estimates in Table 3 keep the same sign and significance, and their magnitudes move by less than a tenth of a standard deviation. Below we answer each comment in turn and give the page, section or table where the change can be found. Where a comment concerned wording, the new sentence is quoted.

## Referee 1

### Comment 1

> Standard errors should be clustered at the industry level, not the prefecture level.

**Response.** We agree and now report both. Table 3, column 2 reports industry-clustered standard errors, and the note to Table 3 explains which level is used in which column. Section 4, page 12, lines 8-14 explains why the two levels give close results in this sample.

### Comment 2

> The paper should show that prefecture fixed effects do not absorb the migration channel.

**Response.** We added an event-study figure as Figure 2 in Section 5, page 18. It reports the dynamic path separately for establishments that hire locally and establishments that hire outside the prefecture.

## Referee 2

### Comment 3

> The sample window should start in 1998, when the first station opened.

**Response.** We do not change the sample window. The administrative records for 1998 and 1999 do not report establishment-level education, so the outcome is undefined in those years; footnote 6 on page 7 now says so. We instead report the 2000 to 2012 window in Appendix Table A2, and the estimate is unchanged.

### Comment 4

> The contribution relative to Faber (2014) is not clear.

**Response.** Section 2, page 5 now contains a paragraph on the difference. Faber studies how the highway network changes local output, while this paper studies the skill mix inside establishments and separates the migration channel from the training channel.
